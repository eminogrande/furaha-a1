# How this app approaches learning

The central loop is: study a small amount, recall without seeing the answer, check feedback, correct mistakes, and return on later days. No one method or app can guarantee language proficiency.

## Design choices and evidence

- **Recall before reveal.** Practice questions and flashcards require an attempt before the answer. Retrieval practice and distributed practice received high utility ratings in Dunlosky et al. (2013), a review of learning techniques: https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266 . This broad learning evidence is not a guarantee of fluency or proof that multiple-choice alone is enough.
- **Review over time.** Previously studied and difficult items return. Research on spacing in second-language learning supports distributed practice: Kim & Webb (2022), https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479 . The app's exact intervals are a simple design choice, not a scientifically optimal prescription.
- **Feedback and another attempt.** Checks explain the answer and offer mistake review. Corrective feedback research in second-language classrooms supports its use; see Lyster & Saito (2010), https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263109990520 . A quiz explanation is not equivalent to a teacher's feedback on speaking or writing.
- **Separate familiarity from demonstrated performance.** Tapping “Got it” is a self-rating, not an objectively correct answer or evidence of CEFR mastery. Attempts and scores describe this app's exercises only.
- **Include language use.** Reading and listening examples are paired with writing and speaking prompts. Model answers support self-comparison; the app does not automatically assess pronunciation or grade open-ended responses. Conversation with another person and qualified feedback remain important.

## Honest boundaries

The German material is an A1-oriented starter, not a complete Goethe preparation course. The Swahili material is beginner practice, not a certified CEFR course. History and proverbs are enrichment. AI-generated German recordings use an approved cloned voice; the current model does not provide verified Swahili speech.

A2–C2 study guides and external resources are not full in-app courses. CEFR describes communicative abilities, not a mandatory grammar checklist: https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages . Exam requirements and practice should be checked with Goethe-Institut: https://www.goethe.de/ins/us/en/spr/prf.html .

Progress stays in the current browser. It is not an exam score, proficiency certification, or prediction that someone will pass.
